Safe Fast Control While a Slow Language Model Thinks

A vision-language model (VLM) can read signs and road workers, but takes hundreds of milliseconds to reply. We combine an event-triggered VLM, a fast controller, and a LiDAR safety filter—so the vehicle stays safe without getting stuck.

01 · Problem & Architecture

Three components, one closed-loop stack

A VLM call takes ~230 ms on a GPU (and seconds over a network), while steering and braking run at 10–50 Hz. Each of our three components solves one failure mode:

Failure Mode 1: Crash

VLM Alone

Waiting hundreds of milliseconds between control updates leaves the vehicle unprotected when a car cuts in or stops ahead.

Failure Mode 2: Freeze

Fast Controller + Static Filter

Braking prevents the crash, but without changing the high-level maneuver, the vehicle sits at zero speed behind a blocked lane forever.

Ours

Event-Triggered VLM + Safety Filter

The safety filter protects every step and reserves 8 m of stopping room while waiting; the VLM wakes only when needed to change maneuvers.

1. Event-Triggered VLM
Queries Qwen3-VL-8B only when the lane is blocked or camera features look unfamiliar. Outputs a discrete maneuver: Stay, Pass, Careful, or Stop.
~230 ms / call
2. Fast Controller
Executes the current maneuver at control rate (trained PPO policy or analytic lane controller) and blends steering across maneuver changes.
10–50 Hz
3. Safety Filter
Uses LiDAR distances to cap speed, guard road boundaries, check late VLM replies against current gaps, and hold an 8 m stopping reserve while a reply is pending.
0.13 ms / step

Why the 8 m stopping reserve matters at 4–5 seconds

Drag the delay slider below. When a reply takes 5+ seconds, a standard safety filter stops right behind the obstacle's bumper—it avoids a crash, but leaves too little room to steer out when the pass command arrives. Holding an 8 m reserve while waiting prevents this trap.

05 · VLM Evaluation on Irregular Scenes (1,200 Episodes)

Where the VLM adds value over LiDAR alone

LiDAR measures geometry, not semantics—so without a VLM, the vehicle drives past a road worker holding a STOP sign 50 out of 50 times. Triggering Qwen3-VL-8B on unfamiliar visual frames halves STOP violations while leaving clean roads untouched.

06 · Limitations

What remains unsolved

Our experiments also highlight clear failure modes that remain open:

1. Zero-shot VLM still misses 25 / 50 STOP signs

Halving violations from 50/50 to 25/50 shows real semantic signal, but 50% compliance is far from sufficient for deployment.

2. Conservative waiting on wet closures

On wet road closures, the VLM avoids collisions (0/50) largely by holding the vehicle stopped for most of the episode (16% route progress).

3. Visual trigger false-alarms on rain

Rain streaks trigger 9.6 VLM calls per episode on empty roads, causing unnecessary caution and reducing progress from 54% to 47%.

4. Reserve helps only at long delays (4+ s)

At typical 0.25 s local GPU latency, the 8 m stopping reserve has minimal effect; its benefit appears when delays exceed 4 seconds.
