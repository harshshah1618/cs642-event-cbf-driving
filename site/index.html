Safe Fast Control While a Slow Language Model Thinks

A vision-language model (VLM) can read signs and road workers, but takes hundreds of milliseconds to reply. We combine an event-triggered VLM, a fast controller, and a LiDAR safety filter—so the vehicle stays safe without getting stuck.

01 · Problem & Architecture

Three components, one closed-loop stack

A VLM call takes ~230 ms on a GPU (and seconds over a network), while steering and braking run at 10–50 Hz. Each of our three components solves one failure mode:

Failure Mode 1: Crash

VLM Alone

Waiting hundreds of milliseconds between control updates leaves the vehicle unprotected when a car cuts in or stops ahead.

Failure Mode 2: Freeze

Fast Controller + Static Filter

Braking prevents the crash, but without changing the high-level maneuver, the vehicle sits at zero speed behind a blocked lane forever.

Ours

Event-Triggered VLM + Safety Filter

The safety filter protects every step and keeps 6.5 m of turning room while waiting; the slow layer wakes only when needed to change maneuvers.

1. Event-Triggered VLM
A LiDAR rule replans when the lane is blocked. Qwen3-VL-8B is called only when camera features look unfamiliar (PatchCore) or LiDAR sees an unexplained static object ahead. Outputs a discrete maneuver: Stay, Pass, Careful, or Stop.
~230 ms / call
2. Fast Controller
Executes the current maneuver at control rate (shielded PPO policy trained on sensor features, or an analytic lane controller) and blends steering across maneuver changes.
10–50 Hz
3. Safety Filter
Uses only the 259-number LiDAR + vehicle-state input to cap speed, guard road boundaries, yield to crossing traffic, check late replies against current gaps, and hold a 6.5 m turning-room reserve while a reply is pending.
0.13 ms / step
02 · Controlled Delay Sweep (200 Untouched Maps × 6 Delays × Reserve On/Off)

Why the turning-room reserve matters from 4 seconds of delay

Drag the delay slider below. When a reply takes 4+ seconds, a standard safety filter stops right behind the obstacle's bumper—it avoids a crash, but leaves too little room to steer out when the pass command arrives. Holding a 6.5 m reserve while waiting prevents this trap.

Simulated Planner Delay 6.0 seconds
Measured at 6 s delay: With 6.5 m reserve = 96.5% of 200 routes finished · Without reserve = 67.5% (paired p = 8.5e-16).
SafeMetaDrive, 200 untouched maps (2000–2199), 2,400 runs. Up to 2 s the reserve makes no significant difference; at 4 s it does (96.0% vs 89.0%, p = 0.007), and at 6–8 s it separates 95–97% from 67%. The road animation above is illustrative; the numbers are measured.

05 · VLM Evaluation on Irregular Scenes (100 Untouched Maps × 8 Scenes × 6 Arms = 4,800 Episodes)

Where the VLM adds value over LiDAR alone

LiDAR measures geometry, not semantics—so without a VLM, the vehicle drives past a road worker holding a STOP sign on 98–99 of 100 maps. The only no-VLM rule that obeys the sign, LiDAR → stop, does so by freezing at every object. Calling Qwen3-VL-8B zero-shot, only when the camera view looks unfamiliar or LiDAR sees an unexplained object, obeys STOP on 82 of 100 maps without needless stops. Every arm uses the same safety filter and controller; only the slow layer differs.

Over all 800 episodes per arm, violations (any collision, or running the STOP sign) fall from 14.1% (normal), 12.8% (always careful) and 12.9% (LiDAR → careful) to 2.9% with the VLM (paired p < 1e-20 each). A ground-truth oracle reaches 0.8%. Larger models did not help: offline, Qwen3-VL-32B and 30B-A3B read the STOP sign less reliably at 15–25 m than the 8B (61% and 33% vs 82%).

06 · Limitations

What remains unsolved

Our experiments also highlight clear failure modes that remain open:

1. The VLM still runs 18 / 100 STOP signs and gets stuck on 13 / 100

The misses are first looks that answer "careful" while the sign shows STOP. The stuck runs come from a conservative release rule (P(proceed) ≥ 0.47 twice in a row), tuned on development maps for this one model.

2. Rain triggers extra calls and caution

Rain streaks and roadside objects trigger 10.7 VLM calls per episode on empty wet roads, lowering progress from 52% to 43%.

3. Reserve and blending help only in specific conditions

The turning-room reserve matters only at 4+ s of delay, and steering blending has no measurable effect on SafeMetaDrive.

4. Simulated perception, heuristic filter

The VLM sees MetaDrive renders with synthetic props and weather. The safety filter is a sampled heuristic, not a certified CBF, and 1–2% of maps still end off-road at tight roundabout entries.
